SSEP Mission Patch

Session 3: Using the Tool To Create New Directions

Goal: Use the tool to find new ideas. Go into groups to discuss the new directions. Finally, go back to pen and paper and draft a new version(s).

01Overview

9.1 · Mon Oct 26 · online

  • Cool reference: The MIT Media Lab identity (2011, TheGreenEyl and E Roon Kang). Three shapes and twelve colors made about 40,000 logos.

Today you’ll use your tool to find new directions for your patch. First, demo your tool in small groups and improve it. Then generate variations, pick two, and write about each one. Bring those and your original sketch to a small group critique. Finally, draw your new patch design.

02In class

100 minutes

  • 20 min: Small group demos of your current patch tool, in breakout rooms. Write down your answers to the following: Is your patch tool creating interesting variations based on your original sketch? Or is it too rigid? Talk with each other to figure out how to improve the tool.
  • 20 min: Execute modifications to your current tool.
  • 25 min: Ideating: Isolate two new designs that you think have potential to contribute to an improved, v2 of your patch. Write about each one and why you chose it (2–3 sentences each). To give you an example, say your tool generated a patch like the one below:

    Concentric circles oscillating out from the center. Orange to blue gradient. Customized, tube-like font. Two elliptical orange orbits. This evokes, to me, the idea of planets revolving in the solar system.

    Why are you describing these variations? So that you can understand what about them you like, and use that information in your new patch design.

  • 25 min: Small group critiques: Take your two new variations + your original sketch and go into small group critiques, in breakout rooms. The purpose of the crits is to work towards a new patch design for yourself. Here are some questions to answer:
    1. What message do you see?
    2. What feels distinctly Baruch?
    3. What feels distinctly space research?
    4. What image would you remember tomorrow?
    5. What would be lost if this symbol disappeared?
    6. What elements of your variants would you like to see in your new design?
  • Now that you have enough information to work on a new patch design (or, if you want, several new designs), begin to draw them out.

03Homework

  • Draw version 2 of your patch design based on the work in class.
  • Now that you have a new design for your patch, build a version 2 of your tool (don’t replace the first version). Use the in-class feedback and any other ideas you have. Remember, you aren’t yet feeding it other artists’ work/designs.
  • Upload your new tool and a photo of your new design to GitHub and link them on your index.
