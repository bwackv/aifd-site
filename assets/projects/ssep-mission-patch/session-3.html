SSEP Mission Patch

Session 3: Using the Tool To Create New Directions

Goal: Use the tool to find new ideas. Then critique and choose.

01Overview

9.1 · Mon Oct 26 · online

  • Cool reference: The MIT Media Lab identity (2011, TheGreenEyl and E Roon Kang). Three shapes and twelve colors made about 40,000 logos.

Today you’re going to use your tool to come up with new directions/ideas for the patch. First you’ll generate a bunch of variations. Then you’ll choose up to three and go back to pen and paper and make new designs.

02In class

100 minutes

  • Small group demos of your current patch tool. Answer the question: Is your patch tool creating interesting variations based on your original sketch? Or is it too rigid? Talk with each other to figure out how to improve the tool.
  • Execute modifications to your current tool.
  • 40 min: Ideating: Take your (up to) three new designs that your tool generates. Write about these new directions (2–3 sentences for each). To give you an example, say our tool generated a patch like the one below:

    Concentric circles oscillating out from the center. Orange to blue gradient. Customized, tube-like font. Two elliptical orange orbits. This evokes, to me, the idea of planets revolving in the solar system.

  • 25 min: Small group critiques: Take your three new ideas + your original sketch and go into small group critiques. The purpose of the crits is to work towards a new patch design for yourself. Here are some questions to answer:
    1. What message do you see?
    2. What feels distinctly Baruch?
    3. What feels distinctly space research?
    4. What image would you remember tomorrow?
    5. What would be lost if this symbol disappeared?
    6. What elements of your variants would you like to see in your new design?
  • 35 min: Now that you have enough information to work on a new patch design (or, if you want, several new designs), begin to draw them out.

03Homework

4–5 hours

  • 4–5 hours: Finish each one by hand, in any medium: pencil, pen and ink, marker, paint, collage, digital illustration. The focus should remain on clarity and communication rather than technical polish.
