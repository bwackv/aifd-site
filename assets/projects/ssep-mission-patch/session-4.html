SSEP Mission Patch

Session 4: Experimentation

Goal: Critique your current version, refine your tool and produce new variants, then experiment.

01In class

9.2 · Wed Oct 28 · in person

Another Media Lab logo

  1. Small group crits of your current version. Write down your answers to the following:
    1. Is your patch tool creating interesting variations based on your version 2 design? Or is it too rigid?
    2. What message do you see?
    3. What feels distinctly Baruch?
    4. What feels distinctly space research?
    5. What image would you remember tomorrow?
    6. What would be lost if this symbol disappeared?
  2. Refine your tool and produce new variants.
  3. Experiment:
    • New inputs: Add in new designs, including other artists’ work.
      1. Ask Gemini to add an upload section to your tool:

        “Add a section to my patch tool where I can upload images of patches. When I upload one, the generated variations should respond to it: its colors, its shapes and its composition.”

      2. Collect your inputs: your v2 drawing, a classmate’s patch, and two patches by other artists (here are two places to start: Patricia Klein’s archive, mission patches).
      3. Upload them one at a time. Generate variations from each and save the ones that work.
    • Animated version: Add in the ability to animate designs.
      1. Decide which parts of your patch move and how. Some ideas:
        • Orbits that revolve
        • Stars that twinkle
        • Text that runs around the edge
        • A slow pulse
        • The patch morphing between versions
      2. Ask Gemini to add animation to your tool:

        “Add animation to my patch tool. [Describe what moves and how.] Add a play/pause button, and buttons to export the animation as a GIF and as a video (WebM).”

      3. Generate a design, play it, and export it.
    • Embroidery mode: Limit the number of colors and thicken thin lines, the way the patch will be stitched.
    • Sheet: Have the tool generate a sheet of 9 variations on one page.
    • Lock: Freeze the parts you like while the rest keeps changing.

02Homework

  • Post version 3 of your tool. It should implement some of the experimental ideas from above. You should also have version 1 and 2 linked on your index.
  • Produce a final patch design. This can be directly from your tool or drawn / created outside of it. Also post an artist statement (150–300 words), consider the following questions:
    1. What does your patch represent?
    2. What symbols or imagery did you choose, and why?
    3. How does the design reflect Baruch’s participation in scientific research and discovery in space?
  • A.I. disclosure for your final design: Were AI tools used during the development of this design? If yes, how were they used? A few sentences is sufficient.
  • Post before the start of class Monday.
