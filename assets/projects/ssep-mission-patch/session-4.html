SSEP Mission Patch

Session 4: Back to the Tool

Goal: Add new designs to your tool, make your patch move, present, and get ready to submit.

01Overview

9.2 · Wed Oct 28 · in person

  • New inputs: Add a section to your tool where you can add in new designs, including other artists’ work. Steps below.
  • Rebuild with A.I.: Update your skills.md and your tool with Gemini, using the new designs.
  • Animated version: Some ideas for what can animate: orbits that revolve, stars that twinkle, text that runs around the edge, a slow pulse, or the patch morphing between versions. Add play/pause and export a short loop. Keep the patch readable; your entry is still a PNG.
  • Embroidery mode: Limit the number of colors and thicken thin lines, the way the patch will be stitched.
  • Sheet: Have the tool generate a sheet of 9 variations on one page.
  • Lock: Freeze the parts you like while the rest keeps changing.
  • Counterpoint: In 2014 Pentagram replaced the Media Lab’s 2011 system with one fixed “ML.” When should a patch stop changing?
  • Turn in:
    1. Up to three entries: PNG (1000 × 1000 px or larger), artist statement, A.I. disclosure.
    2. Your animated patch on GitHub Pages, linked on your index.
    3. Your repo with your skills.md and handoff.md.

02New inputs

  1. Ask Gemini to add an upload section to your tool:

    “Add a section to my patch tool where I can upload images of patches. When I upload one, the generated variations should respond to it: its colors, its shapes and its composition.”

  2. Collect your inputs: your v2 drawing, a classmate’s patch, and two patches by other artists (Patricia Klein’s archive, mission patches).
  3. Upload them one at a time. Generate variations from each and save the ones that work.
  4. Swap: Run a classmate’s patch through your tool, and yours through theirs.
  5. If the tool only picks up colors, give Gemini the inputs, your tool and your skills.md, and have it update the skills.md and rebuild the tool.

03In class

100 minutes

  • 20 min: New inputs: Work through the steps above. Make more versions.
  • 15 min: Rebuild: Update your skills.md with Gemini and rebuild the tool.

    “Here are new designs, my patch tool and its skills.md. Update the skills.md so the tool’s shapes, layout and type draw on these designs, then rebuild the tool from it.”

  • 25 min: Animate: Add moving elements and a play/pause button. Export a short loop.
  • 30 min: Final critique:
    1. Storytelling
    2. Scientific meaning
    3. Representation of Baruch
    4. Clarity and legibility
  • 10 min: Plan your submission.

04Homework

  • Finish the animated version. Post it to GitHub Pages and link it on your index.
  • Make version 3 of your tool. Link v1 and v2 on your index as well.
  • Post your final design and an artist statement (150–300 words) in preparation for submitting:
    1. What does your patch represent?
    2. What symbols or imagery did you choose, and why?
    3. How does the design reflect Baruch’s participation in scientific research and discovery in space?
  • A.I. disclosure for each entry: Were AI tools used during the development of this design? If yes, how were they used? A few sentences is sufficient.
