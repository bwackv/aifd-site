A Style Guide for Your A.I.

A set of instructions to hand off to a.i. so that it creates consistent documents · ART 3041

01What you're making

Rules first, then the page.

You already have a landing page online on GitHub Pages (index.html). In week 1 you wrote down the rules it is supposed to follow. There were a few stylistic decisions like tone and typography, but it was not fully thought-out or comprehensive.

The first thing you should do when constructing this style guide is take this file — or select all on this page — and paste it into Copilot and say something like: “I have to create a style guide for working with you/a.i., here's what my professor has outlined, let's chat about this, what do I have to do?”

Here's a definition from Figma:

“A brand style guide maintains brand consistency and coherence,” explains Leandro. “It gives you helpful templates and guidance on how to use brand elements, including fonts, typography, logo, color palette, and imagery. It's also a key tool to guide messaging and content creation, providing valuable insights into your company's brand voice, tone, and writing style.”

02Write the style guide

The part you write yourself, with a.i. assistance.

I'm not going to hand hold across this entire process, because I want you to ask a.i. for help, see what it returns, and see how it can guide you through it.

In week one you answered a few questions about how your page should look. That was a brief for one page. This is a system — it'll hold up across the semester and beyond.

Write it as instructions, addressed to the A.I. Specific enough that somebody else could follow it without asking you a question.

  • Typography. Which typeface(s)? Serif or sans-serif? Do you have a particular font you love? Go do some research at fonts.adobe.com and fonts.google.com. A good rule of thumb if you want something dynamic: a heading/display font and a more legible body font. How big are your headings? And what size should the body font be?
  • Color. Name them if you want, but be sure to include hex codes. Here's a color picker. How many, and what each one is for — background, text, one accent.
  • Space. Tight or open? How much room around things. How many columns on desktop? How many on tablet? How many on mobile? Ask A.I. about laying out webpages.
  • Images. Should images get an overlay, or some other sort of treatment?

Vague rules produce vague pages!

03Put it in your custom instructions

So you stop repeating yourself.

  1. Open your Copilot custom instructions.
  2. Delete the Tone and Words to avoid lines you filled in earlier.
  3. Paste in your new style guide instructions.
  4. Add this line to the end, exactly as written: Do not display code. Always give me a fully updated and downloadable file complete with all HTML/CSS/JS.
  5. Save it, then open a brand new chat.

Custom instructions apply to every conversation, not just the next one. Once the style guide is in there you stop re-explaining your design decisions every time you start a chat — which is the whole reason to write it down.

04REmake index.html: Begin with a hero image

Generated, not found.

Your page needs one image, across the top. This is called a "hero image". Generate the image using a.i. Copilot can generate images, but sometimes it won't. If that's the case use ChatGPT, Gemini or something else. There are a lot of free options. 

It has to relate to your life somehow. That is as specific as I am going to be — your commute, your apartment, something you own, something you do, what you're into right now, somewhere you go. Take it where you want. In your prompt for the image generation also specify the look/feel. This should "match" the style of the page. Again, what this means is up to you, but you'll know it when you find something that works well. 

Rename the file hero.jpg or hero.png, depending on what filetype it saved as.

If Copilot will not generate images for you, use a free alternative: Google Gemini or ChatGPT.

Things worth putting in the prompt:

  • A style. “Vintage.” “High res.” “Dark academic.” “Chillwave.” “Like Picasso painted it in 2024.”
  • Text or no text. Say which. If you want lettering, expect to fight for it.
  • Realistic or not. Say which.
  • The camera and the angle, if it is a photograph. “35mm, shot from below.”
  • Colors for the image.

The first image will not be the one. Same as the page: say what is wrong with it and ask again.

05Rebuild index.html

Attach your file, then paste the prompt.

Drag your current index.html and your hero image into the Copilot chat box, then paste the prompt below. Copilot reads the file you attach — you never paste code into the chat.

Paste into Copilot 
My style guide is in your custom instructions.
But I'm also going to paste it here:

[paste your style guide]

I have attached my page as it is now, index.html, and my hero image.

Rebuild the page so it follows the style guide, and add two things:
- A background that moves. Written in CSS or JavaScript, inside
  the file. Not a video, not an image.
- A hero image across the top of the page, named hero.jpg.

Keep the rules from before:
- One HTML file. The CSS and the JavaScript go inside it.
- Nothing loaded from outside except the image.
- It has to work on a phone as well as a laptop.
- Keep my Work list and the links I have already filled in.

Give me a downloadable HTML file named index.html.

Name the image file in the prompt and then actually name it that. If your prompt says hero.jpg and you upload IMG_4471.png, the page loads with a broken image and nothing tells you why.

06Put it online

Two files this time.

Your page has always been one self-contained file. The image is the first exception — it has to live in the repository next to the HTML.

  1. In your repository, click Add file, then Upload files. Drag your image in and click Commit changes.
  2. Upload the new index.html the same way. GitHub asks whether to replace the existing file — say yes, then Commit changes.
  3. Wait about a minute and reload your live address.

The steps are the same ones in the GitHub Pages instructions, with an upload in place of a paste.

07Check it

Against the guide, not against your taste.

Open the live address. Did it follow your style guide how you imagined?

Nothing to submit — the live page is the hand-in. It has to be online by the end of class.
