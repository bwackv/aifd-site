05Week

Sep 28 – 30

Swiss typography

with Prof. Mirko Velimirović

Designing with A.I. — generative pedagogy and Swiss typography. A four session module running across weeks 5 and 6, ending in a poster-generating web app of your own.

5.1 · Mon Sep 28 · online

Session 1 — deconstruction, typographic hierarchy and A.I. sourcing

  • Archival research and analysis — the mathematical grid, sans-serif rigor and asymmetric compositions of Josef Müller-Brockmann, Wolfgang Weingart, Typographische Monatsblätter, David Rudnick and Willi Kunz.
    • TM Research Archive
    • MoMA · Josef Müller-Brockmann
    • Sgustok Design · Müller-Brockmann posters
    • MoMA · Wolfgang Weingart
    • SFMOMA · Willi Kunz
    • David Rudnick · posters
    • Tschichold Archive
  • Choose one poster from the archives to build a tool around. You are choosing a poster you like in order to build a tool that lets you design in a similar manner.
  • Typographic recognition exercise — write down your analysis of the type, the color, the hierarchy, the kind of grid, the tone, everything you notice about the poster, into a stream of consciousness text document. Feed that document to the a.i. to produce a skills.md.
  • Upload your reference posters to an a.i. vision model. Have it analyze the typographic hierarchy (H1, H2, body copy ratios), then cross-reference the Google Fonts library and recommend open-source typefaces that match those proportions.
  • The orchestration protocol — two markdown files. skills.md holds the grid system math, the typographic scale and the explicit rules (“left-align text,” “restrict the color palette,” “apply a 12-column grid”). handoff.md holds where the project stands, so a new chat can pick it up when the old one loses the thread. Both are pasted into Gemini at the start of a session, and both are updated as the work goes on.
  • Sketch a paper UI for the poster-generator tool.

HW4 – 5 hours. Source and download the fonts (1 – 2 hrs). Iterate with the a.i. to finalize skills.md and handoff.md, with the typographic scale and hierarchy rules you found spelled out (2 hrs). Make a local project folder and organize the fonts and the inspiration images (1 hr).

5.2 · Wed Sep 30 · in person

Session 2 — prototyping and generating the web engine

  • Review and critique the markdown files you generated.
  • Prototype in the browser — a single full page of HTML / CSS / JS to check that the layout math and the typographic scale hold up. No code snippets, full page please. It has to run the same on a PC and a Mac.
  • Start the build. Hand Gemini your two markdown files, your poster image, and a prompt you wrote in plain language and then had Gemini reformat as an llm prompt.
  • Decide what the tool actually does. Does it take text and randomize a grid? Does the user upload an image and have the tool pull a color palette out of it?
  • Export. The app must produce SVG or PDF you can open and edit in Illustrator — scalable vector math, not a dead end.

HW5 – 6 hours. Pass code back and forth with the a.i. until the basics work (2 hrs). Build the export and verify it — download test files and open them in Illustrator to confirm they are editable (2 hrs). Document the first wave of failures, especially where the type renders one way in the browser and another way in the exported vector (1 – 2 hrs).

06Week

Oct 5 – 7

Swiss typography

with Prof. Mirko Velimirović

6.1 · Mon Oct 5 · online

Session 3 — human in the loop, evaluation and debugging

  • Stress test. Run your app and try to recreate a Müller-Brockmann Tonhalle concert poster.
  • Visual debugging in small groups — share the failed outputs and find the math or logic error behind them. Is the contrast between the display type and the text sizes muddled? Did the a.i. override your sourced Google Fonts with its defaults?
  • A.I. self-correction. Feed the failure screenshots to a separate chat and have it rewrite the prompt and skills.md to fix the structural math and enforce the typographic scale.
  • Write correction prompts that isolate one variable: “the font-size scaling is incorrect, leave the grid alone and fix only the clamp() values.”
  • Export a test SVG or PDF, open it in Illustrator, and check the vector paths survived.

HW4 – 5 hours. Deep iteration — the app moves from barely working to stylistically accurate. Expect to reset the chat with your updated handoff.md at least twice (3 hrs). Generate the final series of test posters, tune the hierarchy, and validate the exports in Illustrator (1 – 2 hrs).

  • Reflection — the difference between designing a poster and designing a system that designs a poster.

Introductory statement

As an introductory statement to this course I want to say that I’ve found that it’s impossible not to become philosophical about the subject of teaching a.i. because much of its proposal is one of automation, of replacing “work” and “effort”. These things, the work, the effort, the process, are ironically the very things that not only turn a.i. into a useful tool, but also reject the idea of a tool that could somehow replace these activities. If you’re going to succeed in your work (in life), you need to enjoy the process. Or at least that is my proposal to all of you. With that being said I want to say a few words that I’ll return to throughout the semester:

Imagine
Daydream or philosophize or think deeply and if your wildest imagination comes up with an idea, perhaps “a.i.” can enable you to bring it to life whereas before there might have been some difficult-to-surmount roadblocks.
Articulate
You might have an idea in your mind’s eye, but can you spell it out for others? In the case of this course, that requires you to be as specific as possible when you work with a.i. tools. The better your plan, the better the work it will do. Also, at its base you’re still working with a machine. They take things literally, or work better when you spell things out literally. It’s worth watching a bit of this video about instructing someone to make a PB&J sandwich.
Taste
What makes something good? An ever moving target but it’s one that separates the amateurs from the pros and it will separate the slop work from the great work in this class and beyond. What do you like and why? Can you articulate that?

A quote to end on:

“This emphasis on pleasure runs counter to the dominant story we are currently being sold about creative work. There is, says Shallcrass, ‘so much talk about automation’ and artificial intelligence: a discourse that frames making as a chore and human involvement as a problem to be solved. To automate is to assume that the output is what matters, and the process merely what it takes to get there. But, he asks, what about those of us who enjoy our work—who find meaning in the production of letterforms, or music, or films? Shallcrass made Constrictor because the process of making the letterforms contributed to his understanding of them. The joy is not incidental to the work, it is the work.”

Frere-Jones Type — Designing Constrictor

I like this quote because it speaks to the joy of making. I derive joy from making and also from experiencing work where I sense the presence of that joy and of the human (hand/mind/voice/eye) being behind it.
