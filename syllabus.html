02Course introduction

Whatever your stance on it, it is valuable to know about this technology and how to use it.

“In this new world, creation is cheap, craft is automatable, and everyone is a beginner. The ultimate differentiator will be the creator’s perspective, taste, and judgment. The software design education for our current moment must prioritize this above all else.”

AI Software Design Studio (Fall 2024), RISD — Kelin Carolyn Zhang

Technologists are decrying the disappearance of the apprentice. Entry level jobs will vanish, these workers’ tasks subsumed by artificial intelligence technologies. A unique opportunity presents itself in this level playing field: students, recent graduates and seasoned professionals are all approaching these technologies at the same time, with the same level of expertise.

Understanding how A.I. tools work and how to integrate them (or not) into your workflow and creative practice as an artist/designer is critical to positioning yourself within the workforce and the larger design world. The way that students interact with these tools will facilitate executive level thinking that I hope will make one more prepared for the post-college workforce.

Students have a unique opportunity to explore these technologies, their ethics and their creative potentials with their classmates and professors and generate (pun intended) new ways of utilizing and approaching the paradigms. As a workshop, the course will change as we tackle the topics and discover new avenues and ideas.

03Additional details description

ART 3041, Topics in AI and Design Studio, is a workshop and forum. Students begin the semester codifying their own design principles and embedding them into their a.i. tool(s) of choice. Baruch uses Microsoft Copilot, but you can use others as well. Students will assume the role of creative director and collaborator with these A.I. tools, utilizing and working with them in various creative projects.

Students will gain an understanding of the history behind machine learning and the quest for “artificial intelligence”. This historical perspective naturally leads to a consideration of larger issues. A.I. technologies are displacing human-centered work, straining the energy grid and dividing our vision of the future. These ethical, social and environmental topics will be discussed and addressed through student work, writing, reading and discussion.

Whatever conclusion one reaches about these technologies, this professor recognizes the university as a place to study and gain knowledge about them before going out into a work force that demands familiarity.

04Course objectives

  • Learn the history of A.I.
  • Understand how you want to use A.I. in your own practice
  • Understand how to get the most out of these tools
  • Develop the judgment to evaluate two outputs and articulate why one is better
  • Become proficient in what these tools currently offer, so that you are positioned to keep pace with a technology that keeps accelerating
  • Explore, experiment with and test this technology
  • Discuss and grapple with historical and contemporary issues of A.I., including ethical, personal, economic, philosophical and political
  • Make something you are excited about

Introductory statement

As an introductory statement to this course I want to say that I’ve found that it’s impossible not to become philosophical about the subject of teaching a.i. because much of its proposal is one of automation, of replacing “work” and “effort”. These things, the work, the effort, the process, are ironically the very things that not only turn a.i. into a useful tool, but also reject the idea of a tool that could somehow replace these activities. If you’re going to succeed in your work (in life), you need to enjoy the process. Or at least that is my proposal to all of you. With that being said I want to say a few words that I’ll return to throughout the semester:

Imagine
Daydream or philosophize or think deeply and if your wildest imagination comes up with an idea, perhaps “a.i.” can enable you to bring it to life whereas before there might have been some difficult-to-surmount roadblocks.
Articulate
You might have an idea in your mind’s eye, but can you spell it out for others? In the case of this course, that requires you to be as specific as possible when you work with a.i. tools. The better your plan, the better the work it will do. Also, at its base you’re still working with a machine. They take things literally, or work better when you spell things out literally. It’s worth watching a bit of this video about instructing someone to make a PB&J sandwich.
Taste
What makes something good? An ever moving target but it’s one that separates the amateurs from the pros and it will separate the slop work from the great work in this class and beyond. What do you like and why? Can you articulate that?

A quote to end on:

“This emphasis on pleasure runs counter to the dominant story we are currently being sold about creative work. There is, says Shallcrass, ‘so much talk about automation’ and artificial intelligence: a discourse that frames making as a chore and human involvement as a problem to be solved. To automate is to assume that the output is what matters, and the process merely what it takes to get there. But, he asks, what about those of us who enjoy our work—who find meaning in the production of letterforms, or music, or films? Shallcrass made Constrictor because the process of making the letterforms contributed to his understanding of them. The joy is not incidental to the work, it is the work.”

Frere-Jones Type — Designing Constrictor

I like this quote because it speaks to the joy of making. I derive joy from making and also from experiencing work where I sense the presence of that joy and of the human (hand/mind/voice/eye) being behind it.
